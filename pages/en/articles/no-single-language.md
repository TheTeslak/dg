Accessibility as a design principle is close to my heart. In this blog, I thought through keyboard navigation and screen reader support for blind users

[I articulated](https://t.me/TesNot/7) a similar thought about duplicating content when starting my Telegram blog

Going multilingual extends the same principle

Translating into every language is hard. I picked the popular ones and those where people I know can point out nuances:

- Russian as my native language
- English as the international language
- <GlossaryTerm term="Spanish" definition="Español neutro — a version without regional bias, understood from Spain to Argentina">Spanish</GlossaryTerm> and Portuguese, which I am picking up little by little
- French, which I had in school
- and German!

+ it's just interesting to see the differences

[I want to be understood in major languages, but I do not understand Asian languages at all. After studying the nuances of CJK, I decided not to risk translating without an editor.]{.muted}

Built-in browser translators are weak

And with just one language it is hard to get into someone else’s language bubble

If an article is not translated into the selected language, the site offers an available version

![Multilingual Teslaks|no-caption](/images/multilang.avif)

## Translation methodology

A long prompt translates the text in chunks and explains the differences between phrasing options. I read closely and choose, then pass it to another AI — and so on in a loop, weeding out inaccuracies one by one

Facts and names are localized [in not-yet-published articles]

Drafts are mostly in Russian, so I translate them. Though I am writing a new article on design in English

I edit the English version myself; the rest get less attention because I don't know them well enough
